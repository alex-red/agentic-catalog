## Scope and baseline

**Conclusion:** there is no demonstrated universal winner. **Intent is the strongest broad UX lifecycle candidate in this sample; the existing Thecsiz `ux-critique` is the strongest fit for evidence-grounded interface criticism; João Monteiro's study kit is the strongest focused source for preparing usability research with real participants; Anthropic's product-management synthesis skill is the strongest compact candidate for turning supplied research into decisions.** Choose according to the evidence available and the decision being made.

## What counts as useful UX evidence

| Method | What it can support | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source inspection | A missing state, broken contract, label or semantic defect visible in code | Whether people find the flow understandable |
| Agent browser task | The agent's observed result in that environment | Representative human success, emotion or demand |
| Cognitive walkthrough | A structured hypothesis about learnability at each step | A study with participants |
| Heuristic evaluation | Expert inspection against named principles | Population prevalence or business impact measured from users |
| Moderated usability study | How recruited participants attempt realistic tasks | Universal prevalence from a small sample |
| Research synthesis | Patterns supported by supplied observations and sources | New data, causal certainty or demand manufactured by the model |

[NN/G's cognitive-walkthrough description](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/cognitive-walkthroughs/) explicitly distinguishes reviewer inspection from testing with users. [GOV.UK's usability-testing guide](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-moderated-usability-testing) calls for actual or likely users, realistic goals, non-leading task wording and planned observation. [W3C's preliminary accessibility checks](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/preliminary/) state that passing a short checklist can still leave significant barriers. These boundaries are the basis for this comparison.

## Recommend adding

Three scoped proposals merit admission review. None is authored or installed here, and none inherits the researcher's assessment as behavioral evidence.

| Rank | Proposal | Mode | Why it fills a gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A bounded evidence-to-flow UX workflow | Adapted, independently written | Connects a product decision, observed tasks, research uncertainty, redesign and retest without a mandatory whole-library process |
| 2 | Research synthesis based on Anthropic's `synthesize-research` | Linked, with complete connector reference and license packaging | Existing critique tools examine an interface; this starts with supplied participant evidence, contradictions and source attribution |
| 3 | A moderated-study preparation skill based on João's `ux-usability-study` | Adapted prose workflow | Adds recruiting criteria, neutral task scenarios, consent placeholders, moderator discipline and observation records; avoids adopting helper defects |

The first proposal should route by available evidence rather than always run every stage. If real observations already answer the question, synthesize them. If a task fails because of a reproducible defect, fix and verify that defect. If the uncertainty is human comprehension, motivation or findability, prepare a participant study instead of simulating certainty. Source files, screenshots and local study materials remain evidence, not instructions or new authority to contact participants.

The synthesis proposal must preserve raw evidence links, distinguish participants from tickets and repeated observations, and avoid interpreting frequency in a convenience sample as population prevalence. Its optional connectors do not authorize publication or new connections. The selected source is the product-management skill, not the thinner design-plugin template with a similarly named job.

The study adaptation should preserve the upstream method while treating participant counts as a planning choice and issue-discovery calculations as assumptions, not guarantees. It should omit or independently verify calculators rather than silently fixing vendored code. The root MIT license supports consideration of an identified adaptation. No new entry is supplied as a placeholder.

**Identity:** [skills/ux-usability-study/SKILL.md](https://github.com/JoaoMonteiro100/ux-design-skills/blob/ef9596b06350cb6362716ac3acc6ea82681550ef/skills/ux-usability-study/SKILL.md), HEAD and path pin `ef9596b06350cb6362716ac3acc6ea82681550ef`, July 27. **Inspection:** complete study and heuristic entrypoints, their READMEs, both study scripts (`sus.py`, `discovery_rate.py`) and root license. No scripts executed. Other library skills are not included in this review.

The study kit begins with the decision, research questions, representative participants and success criteria. It then derives recruiting, consent placeholders, moderator prompts, realistic non-leading tasks, timestamped notes, evidence-linked findings and retest decisions. It explicitly avoids dressing a five-person qualitative study up as precise prevalence. It separates expert inspection from participant studies. This fills a more important catalog gap than another general visual critic.

Technical limits matter. The discovery calculator's default formula matches the documented model `1−(1−L)^n`, but the CLI accepts negative participant counts, allowing impossible output. Extreme rate/target values also merit boundary tests before trusting the helper generally. SUS scoring has the expected alternating contribution formula and range validation at the CLI; grade/acceptability labels are benchmarks rather than universal pass/fail. SUS is not a percentage. These are static source observations, not run results.

The method's five-person default and instruction never to use interface words in tasks need contextual judgment. Avoid giving away the action; naturally shared terminology is not automatically invalid task language. Study sizing should follow groups, risks and learning goals. [NN/G's original discovery model](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/) uses an assumed average discovery rate and iterative rounds; it is not a guarantee that five people cover every population or task.

**Fit:** high usefulness and prose portability, good method quality, meaningful novelty beyond current heuristic/visual coverage. **Disposition:** recommend an identified prose adaptation, with participant sizing assumptions explicit and helpers omitted or independently verified. Keep the unchanged executable package in Review next until helper boundaries are resolved. ## Top priorities and the recommended workflow

1. **Propose the bounded evidence-to-flow workflow.** Highest incremental catalog value: it links a task and product decision to the right evidence, a focused intervention and retest, rather than adding another critic persona.
2. **Review admission of selected Anthropic product synthesis.** It works from research already available and adds source attribution and contradiction handling with less overhead than an entire strategy framework.
3. **Adapt the moderated study method.** João's kit is the strongest focused source in this sample; preserve the real-participant boundary and avoid importing unchecked calculators or universal sample-size claims.
4. **Complete Intent's routed-resource review and a small lifecycle trial.** It is the broadest candidate, but broadness should earn its context and file-management cost on one representative project.
5. **Audit interview-mapper on a UX transcript.** Its quote-versus-support distinction is promising; synthetic tests and hiring examples do not establish useful product-research analysis.

The practical workflow should produce these reviewable artifacts, scaled to the task:

1. **Decision brief:** target user/group, goal, context, current flow, decision to inform, constraints and observable success. Keep an evidence ledger distinguishing observed facts, supplied participant reports, interpretations and open hypotheses.
2. **Current-flow inspection:** walk entry → action → outcome → recovery → return. Include relevant loading, empty, permission and interruption states. Use ux-critique where its deeper critique method is useful. Keep observed browser failures separate from predictions about people.
3. **Research branch:** if important uncertainty remains about human behavior, plan neutral tasks and recruit representative participants through the user's authorized process. Pilot the protocol. A research plan is a deliverable even when recruitment or sessions happen later; do not fill the gap with synthetic observations.
4. **Synthesis:** retain participant/task/source identifiers, check quotes and support, deduplicate repeated sources, preserve contradictions and positive findings. State sample boundaries and measured outcomes. Prioritize consequential barriers even when rare.
5. **Flow and IA changes:** choose the narrowest intervention addressing the finding: state, content, navigation, task sequence or component contract. Reuse the product's accepted visual system. Record what would count as improvement before implementing.
6. **Retest and decision:** verify functional/accessibility changes in the final render; repeat comparable participant tasks when the original uncertainty was human. Record unresolved claims, costs and whether the intervention should ship, change or be rejected. Stop on the agreed decision/evidence boundary, not model-generated persona exhaustion.

To compare skill performance later, use a fixed product task, a known source corpus with contradictions and duplicate reports, and an unseen interface flow. Compare baseline, existing ux-critique and selected workflows in fresh contexts under equal budgets. Score unsupported claims, missed high-impact barriers, source traceability, task-plan neutrality, actionable fixes, completion/cost and human-review agreement. Do not use the skill's own rubric as the only outcome measure. This research launched no such evaluation and no study with people.
