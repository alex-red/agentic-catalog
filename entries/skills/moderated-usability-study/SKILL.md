# Moderated usability study

Produce the study materials or analysis needed for the user's current decision. A prepared protocol is a useful deliverable; it is not a completed study. Use actual session records for findings about participants. Treat a simulated walkthrough as inspection, labelled separately.

## Frame the decision

Identify the decision, target users, product version, relevant tasks and unanswered questions. Reuse supplied context. Distinguish what needs observation from what an existing recording, support record or reproducible defect already establishes.

Choose participants who do or plausibly would perform the task. Specify relevant experience, access needs and exclusions without substituting demographic stereotypes for behavior. Choose the number and grouping for the learning goal, risk and available time; no fixed small sample guarantees issue coverage or statistical significance. Record important groups that will remain unrepresented.

## Prepare a usable protocol

Include only the materials requested or needed for the next session:

- **Participant criteria and screener:** questions about actual circumstances and behavior, with selection criteria that do not reveal the desired answer.
- **Session introduction:** purpose, voluntary participation, ability to stop, and organization-supplied consent, recording and data-handling terms. Mark missing organizational terms as questions rather than inventing commitments.
- **Task scenarios:** realistic context, goal and starting conditions. Define an observable completion criterion and a stopping condition for each task. Avoid naming the control or route being tested; naturally shared product terminology is acceptable when it does not give away the answer.
- **Moderator guide:** neutral opening questions, task order, prompts such as “What would you do next?”, when to remain silent, and how to handle requests for help. Log assistance because it changes what unassisted success means.
- **Observation sheet:** participant code, task, product version, timestamp or record locator, action or exact words, outcome, assistance and separate interpretation.

Pilot the protocol for unclear scenarios, broken setup, leading wording and session length. A moderator or agent pilot checks the protocol and environment; keep its results separate from participant evidence.

Preparing a screener or introduction does not itself authorize recruiting, messaging, recording, uploading data or connecting services. Follow the user's actual research scope and existing permissions for those actions.

## Observe and analyze

During authorized sessions, record what participants do and say before interpreting it. Do not teach the intended path before observing an attempt. Preserve failures, workarounds, positive cases and moderator interventions. Label missing records and ambiguous quotes rather than reconstructing them.

For each finding, retain:

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Claim | The task difficulty or successful behavior observed |
| Evidence | Participant codes and timestamps, quotations or source locations |
| Coverage | Distinct participants exposed to the relevant task and those exhibiting the behavior |
| Consequence | Blocked outcome, recovery effort or other observed effect |
| Interpretation | Proposed explanation, contradictory cases and plausible alternatives |
| Next decision | Change, retain, investigate further or retest, with the supporting reason |

Count distinct participants rather than repeated comments or tickets. Keep denominators tied to task exposure. Small or convenience samples support situated findings, not population prevalence. A severe barrier can merit action even when observed once; frequency is not the sole priority rule.

If a standardized questionnaire is requested, preserve its wording and scoring method and verify any calculation used. SUS scores are not percentages. This skill supplies no discovery-rate or questionnaire calculator.

## Deliver the next decision

For preparation, return a session-ready protocol and unresolved prerequisites. For analysis, report sample and task coverage, evidence-linked findings, contradictions, recommendations and remaining uncertainty. Distinguish observed outcomes from explanations and expected benefits.

For a proposed change, identify what a retest should observe and which users or situations it needs to cover. Do not claim improvement until the appropriate evidence exists. If there are no participant records, stop at preparation or inspection hypotheses and say what remains untested.
